Inventory Policy Engine — Modeling Details & Assumptions

The rationale behind the reorder-point / safety-stock model in 14_inventory_policy.ipynb. Version 1 uses a conformal approach: a point forecast plus the empirical distribution of rolling-origin backtest errors. This documents the method, every assumption it rests on, why it is closed-form (no LP/MILP), and the upgrade path.

1 Scope & where it sits

Step 7 (planning engines), first component. Consumes the twins + forecast backtest + planning_config; produces recommendations.

The engine turns detected inventory abnormalities into quantified replenishment recommendations. For each in-scope material × plant it computes an order-up-to policy (reorder point / order-up-to level, safety stock, suggested order quantity) and writes recommended_action onto the open inventory events. It reads:

  • Point forecast and its rolling-origin backtest errors — from the demand forecast (02).
  • Lead time — from the procurement twin (purchased items) or the production twin (produced items).
  • Inventory position (on-hand + on-order) — on-order = open POs (purchased) or open production/WIP (produced), from the inventory, procurement & production twins.
  • Policy parameters (service level, MOQ, ...) — from monitoring.planning_config.
Scope — by demand independence (not purchased-vs-made)

A reorder point / order-up-to is an independent-demand technique, so the engine covers stocked items that have a sales demand forecast. How the target is replenished then depends on sourcing:

  • Independent + purchased (e.g. green beans sold bulk) → replenishment_mode = PO, supplier lead time.
  • Independent + produced (e.g. roasted 500g finished goods) → replenishment_mode = PRODUCTION; the order-up-to level is a master-production-schedule (MPS) signal handed to MRP / finite scheduling, production lead time.
  • Dependent demand only (components/intermediates consumed in production — packaging, dairy, roasted bulk) → MRP_DEPENDENT: no reorder point. Their demand is derived by MRP (BOM explosion of the finished-goods plan), not forecast statistically.

For an item with both independent and dependent demand (green beans are sold and roasted), this engine covers only the independent (sales) portion; MRP adds the dependent portion.

Key modeling assumptions (made explicit)
  • Primary supplier = largest PO volume. For purchased items, lead time and the supplier named in the PO recommendation come from the single highest-volume supplier per material × plant — ignores dual-sourcing, supplier splits, and cheaper/faster alternates.
  • Lead time = mean recorded actual, rounded up to whole weeks (min 1). L = max(1, ⌈lead_days/7⌉) from avg_lead_time_days (procurement) or avg_actual_lead_time_hours (production). Lead-time variability is ignored (the available std is not used) — see §8.
  • Inventory position = on-hand + on-order; backorders excluded (§3.6).
  • μ_X from the sales forecast only — for items with mixed demand it omits dependent consumption (added by MRP).
  • Cycle service level target (not fill rate); EOQ informational, computed only where a unit cost exists.
Version 1 — conformal reorder point (point forecast + empirical residual quantile)

The reorder point is the service-level quantile of lead-time demand, obtained distribution-free: take the point forecast over the window and add the empirical quantile of the cumulative \(P\)-step forecast errors observed in a rolling-origin backtest.

\[ SS=\max\!\big(0,\ \hat Q_{\text{SL}}(e_P)\big),\qquad S^\star=\mu_X+SS,\qquad Q_{\text{sugg}}=\max\!\big(0,\ S^\star-\text{position}\big) \]

where \(e_P=\big(\sum_{P}\text{actual}\big)-\big(\sum_{P}\text{forecast}\big)\) are the aggregate errors from each backtest origin, and \(\hat Q_{\text{SL}}\) is their split-conformal quantile — the \(\lceil (n{+}1)\,\text{SL}\rceil\)-th smallest of the \(n\) errors (§3.4). The buffer is floored at 0: a negative quantile (systematic over-forecasting) never pushes \(S^\star\) below \(\mu_X\).

Sources: \(\mu_X\) = the per-period forecast summed over the window (\(\sum_{t\le P}\hat d_t\), from the forecast time-series); \(e_P\) sets from an extended 02 rolling-origin backtest (emitted for all horizons \(1\ldots H\); 14 picks \(h=P\) per series); \(P=L+R\) with \(L=\max(1,\lceil\)avg_lead_time_days\(/7\rceil)\) weeks and \(R\) = the twin refresh cadence (§2); position \(=\)on_hand_qty+on-order (open POs for purchased, open production/WIP for produced; backorders deliberately excluded, §3.6); \(\text{SL}=\)service_level; round to order_multiple; MOQ applies only when an order is actually triggered.

Why this as v1: needs only point forecasts (no probabilistic model), is model-agnostic, and is correct for skewed demand (a quantile, not \(\mu\pm z\sigma\)). Deferred (§8): lead-time variability, time-series conformal (EnbPI/ACI), heteroscedastic/relative residuals, generative modelling for intermittent SKUs, real-time feeds (\(R\to0\)), EOQ, fill-rate targets.

2 Policy choice: the review period tracks data freshness

One model, parameterized by \(R\). Today \(R\) = the twin refresh cadence; as the feeds go real-time, \(R\to0\).

A stockout can only occur during the protection interval \(P\): the window between deciding to replenish and the replenishment being on the shelf.

  • Continuous review target state — the position is watched live and an order fires the instant it crosses the reorder point; exposure is only the lead time, \(P=L\).
  • Periodic review v1 effective mode — the position is only observed every \(R\) days; exposure is \(P=L+R\) (more buffer).

The twins are currently fed in batch / micro-batch, so even though the agent reacts to events, the effective review period is the refresh cadence: v1 sets \(R=\)review_period_days = the twin refresh interval. Claiming \(P=L\) on batch-fed twins would overstate the achieved service level. The conformal residuals are simply measured at the corresponding horizon \(P=L+R\).

Roadmap: real-time feeds shrink the buffer The pipelines are planned to move from micro-batch to real-time, so the twins always carry the freshest state. Nothing in the model changes — as the refresh cadence tightens, \(R\to0\), \(P\to L\), residuals are measured at a shorter horizon, and safety stock falls. The streaming upgrade is therefore a quantifiable service-level / working-capital improvement, not just plumbing.
3 The model

Reorder to the service-level quantile of lead-time demand; get that quantile from backtest residuals.

3.1  The target — reorder point = lead-time-demand quantile

Base-stock / newsvendor theory: the optimal order-up-to level is the critical-fractile quantile of the lead-time-demand (LTD) distribution \(F_{LTD}\):

\[ S^\star=F_{LTD}^{-1}(\text{SL})\quad(\text{cycle service}),\qquad S^\star=F_{LTD}^{-1}\!\Big(\tfrac{C_u}{C_u+C_o}\Big)\ (\text{cost-based}). \]

Derivation (cost form): expected cost \(C(S)=C_o\,E[(S-X)^+]+C_u\,E[(X-S)^+]\); setting \(C'(S)=0\) gives \(F_X(S^\star)=\tfrac{C_u}{C_u+C_o}\). For a cycle service level SL, no-stockout means \(X\le S\), so \(F_X(S^\star)=\text{SL}\) by definition — the same equation with the target named directly. So the whole task is estimating \(F_{LTD}\)'s quantile; v1 does that with a conformal residual estimate.

3.2  Protection interval \(P\)

\[ P=L+R,\qquad L=\max\!\Big(1,\ \big\lceil \texttt{avg\_lead\_time\_days}/7 \big\rceil\Big)\ \text{weeks} \]

\(R\) = review period = the twin refresh cadence (§2; real-time feeds \(\Rightarrow R\to0\Rightarrow P\to L\)). The \(\max(1,\lceil\cdot\rceil)\) guard matters: plain rounding sends lead times under ~3.5 days to \(P=0\), which silently degenerates the policy (\(\mu_X=0\), empty error window) for the fastest suppliers. \(L\) is fixed; lead-time variability is a documented upgrade (§8).

3.3  The centre — expected demand over \(P\)

\[ \mu_X=\sum_{t=\text{now}}^{\text{now}+P}\hat d_t \]

The per-period forecast summed over the window — the forecast time-series over the first \(P\) periods (demand_twin_metrics_timeseries / gold.demandplan). This is readily available and captures within-window trend/seasonality; a flat weekly rate \(\times P\) would be the cruder alternative.

3.4  The buffer — conformal empirical quantile of backtest errors v1 method

Rather than assume a distribution for demand, measure how wrong the forecast has been over the window, empirically. In the rolling-origin backtest, at each origin form the cumulative \(P\)-step forecast and actual and record the aggregate error \(e_P=\sum_P\text{actual}-\sum_P\text{forecast}\). The buffer is the conformal quantile of those errors, and the reorder point adds it to the forecast:

\[ \hat Q_{\text{SL}}=e_{(\lceil (n+1)\,\text{SL}\rceil)},\qquad SS=\max\!\big(0,\ \hat Q_{\text{SL}}\big),\qquad S^\star=\mu_X+SS \]

with \(e_{(1)}\le\cdots\le e_{(n)}\) the sorted errors. Two deliberate refinements over a plain percentile:

  • Finite-sample conformal quantile. The plain empirical percentile systematically underestimates the true tail when \(n\) is small — you have likely never observed the real extreme. Split conformal corrects this by taking the \(\lceil (n{+}1)\,\text{SL}\rceil\)-th order statistic, i.e. treating the next error as an unseen \((n{+}1)\)-th point. At \(n=100\), SL 95% that is the 96th vs ~95th value (negligible); at \(n=30\) it is the sample maximum. If \(\lceil (n{+}1)\,\text{SL}\rceil>n\), there are too few origins for that SL (needs \(n\ge\text{SL}/(1-\text{SL})\): 19 for 95%, 99 for 99%) — v1 takes the max and sets an insufficient_calibration flag (fallback: pooled relative residuals, §7).
  • Floor at zero. A negative quantile (systematic over-forecasting) is statistically a legitimate bias correction, but a "negative safety stock" destroys planner trust — v1 floors \(SS\) at 0 (conservative; only raises achieved coverage).

Worked mini-example. Weekly actuals [10,12,9,11,14,8,13,10,12,15,9,11], forecast = 11/wk, \(P=3\). Rolling 3-week aggregate errors (actual sum − 33): [-2,-1,1,0,2,-2,2,4,3,2], so \(n=10\). At SL 95%, \(\lceil 11\times0.95\rceil=11>10\): too few origins, so take the max — \(SS=4\), \(S^\star=33+4=37\) — and flag insufficient_calibration (95% needs \(n\ge19\)). No \(\sigma\), no \(z\), no Normal assumption — just an order statistic.

Why measure at the aggregate horizon Measuring the cumulative \(P\)-step error (not per-period errors scaled by \(\sqrt P\)) bakes in autocorrelation automatically and needs no independence assumption. It also absorbs the fact that these are forecast errors (what we care about), not raw demand variation. Empirical quantiles handle skew for free.
This is conformal prediction Mechanically this is split (inductive) conformal prediction with the backtest as the calibration set: the aggregate residuals are conformity scores, and \(\hat Q_{\text{SL}}\) (the \(\lceil (n{+}1)\text{SL}\rceil\)-th order statistic) is a one-sided conformal bound. Only point forecasts are required — the "probabilistic" part lives in the residual distribution, estimated empirically (or fit parametrically). Time-series data violate exchangeability, so strict coverage guarantees need a time-series conformal variant (EnbPI, ACI) — an §8 upgrade.

3.5  The Normal special case (for reference)

If the aggregate residuals are roughly symmetric/Normal, the empirical quantile reduces to the familiar closed form with \(\sigma_X=\operatorname{std}(\{e_P\})\):

\[ S^\star=\mu_X+z_{\text{SL}}\,\sigma_X,\qquad z_{\text{SL}}=\Phi^{-1}(\text{SL}),\qquad SS=z_{\text{SL}}\,\sigma_X. \]

This is only the symmetric special case of §3.4; v1 uses the empirical quantile so it stays correct for skewed demand. (The cruder textbook form estimates \(\sigma_X=\sqrt{(L+R)\sigma_d^2+\mu_d^2\sigma_L^2}\) from historical demand + lead-time variability; we prefer the measured backtest residuals.)

3.6  Suggested order quantity

\[ Q_{\text{raw}}=S^\star-\text{position};\qquad Q_{\text{sugg}}=\begin{cases}0, & Q_{\text{raw}}\le 0\\[2pt] \max\!\big(\operatorname{round}_{\texttt{order\_multiple}}(Q_{\text{raw}}),\ \texttt{min\_order\_qty}\big), & Q_{\text{raw}}>0\end{cases} \]

MOQ is conditional: the floor applies only when an order is actually triggered (\(Q_{\text{raw}}>0\)). The engine never orders MOQ purely because of the floor — no order needed means no order. Rounding / MOQ reflect case packs and supplier minimums.

Inventory position = on-hand + on-order (on_hand_qty + on-order, where on-order = open_po_qty for purchased items or open production / WIP for produced items) — counting on-order stock avoids re-ordering what is already in transit or in production. The textbook position also subtracts backorders; v1 deliberately omits that term: gold.outbounddeliveries covers only 3 of 21 sold materials, so an ordered − delivered backlog is wildly overstated for the other 18 (see the architecture doc's data-realism gaps). Reinstate the backorder term once delivery coverage is completed in the synthetic data.

3.7  EOQ — a cost trade-off (informational)

\[ Q^\star=\sqrt{\tfrac{2DK}{h}}\qquad (D=\text{annual demand},\ K=\texttt{ordering\_cost},\ h=\texttt{holding\_cost\_rate}\times\text{unit cost}). \]

Minimizes ordering + holding cost; informational here (order size comes from the order-up-to gap), and the fixed \(Q\) in a true \((s,Q)\) policy. Needs a unit cost for \(h\); computed only where a cost exists, else null.

7 Assumptions & limitations
Stationary residual distribution Pooling backtest errors assumes future errors resemble past ones. Regime shifts (new pricing, demand breaks) invalidate the pooled quantile.
Homoscedasticity Pooling absolute errors assumes spread is independent of the demand level. If bigger demand → bigger errors, use relative residuals (error/forecast) or bucket by level/season (§8).
Exchangeability (time series) Split-conformal coverage guarantees assume exchangeability, which autocorrelated series violate — so v1's coverage is empirical, not guaranteed. Time-series conformal (EnbPI/ACI) restores it (§8).
Overlapping origins — nominal \(n\) overstates the evidence Adjacent rolling origins share most of their \(P\)-week window, so the \(e_P\) values are autocorrelated and the effective sample size sits well below the origin count. v1 keeps all overlapping origins — deduplicating to non-overlapping windows would divide \(n\) by \(P\) and estimate the tail from almost nothing, which is strictly worse: the overlapped estimator is still consistent, just noisier than \(n\) independent points would be. Mitigations: the conformal \((n{+}1)\) correction already pushes conservative; the SL is capped by origin count (\(n\ge\text{SL}/(1-\text{SL})\), else insufficient_calibration + fallback); block-bootstrap / EnbPI (§8) is the principled fix.
Cold start / sparse series → pooled relative residuals Series with too little backtest history get no per-series quantile. v1 fallback: pool relative errors (error ÷ forecast level) across the material group, take the conformal quantile of the pooled set, and rescale by the series' \(\mu_X\). Relative — not absolute — errors, so high- and low-volume SKUs are not mixed on incompatible scales. Fail-safe beats emitting nothing: the row is flagged, never silently dropped.
Fixed lead time; cycle service; independent-demand scope v1 fixes \(L\) (uses the mean lead time, no lead-time variability), targets cycle service level \(\alpha\) (not fill rate \(\beta\)), and is scoped to items with independent (sales) demand — purchased sold items (PO) and produced sold items (order-up-to as an MPS signal). Dependent-demand items (components/intermediates consumed in production) are excluded and left to MRP; see §1.
8 Open decisions & roadmap

Upgrade ladder from the conformal v1 (each additive, no rework):

  • recommended Have 02 emit the per-series cumulative-error sets for all horizons \(1\ldots H\) from its existing rolling-origin backtest.
  • Time-series conformal (EnbPI / ACI, block bootstrap) for coverage under autocorrelation & drift — also the principled fix for overlapping-origin correlation (§7).
  • Heteroscedastic / conditional residuals (relative errors, seasonal buckets).
  • Lead-time variability — random \(P\) (sample \(L\)); reintroduces the \(\mu_d^2\sigma_L^2\) effect.
  • Generative probabilistic modelling (Poisson/NB/Croston + Monte-Carlo) for intermittent / lumpy SKUs where point-forecast residuals are ill-defined; classify by ADI / CV².
  • Real-time twin feeds — \(R\to0\Rightarrow P\to L\): shorter protection interval, residuals measured at a shorter horizon, less safety stock (a quantifiable win from the streaming upgrade).
  • Fill-rate (\(\beta\)) target; EOQ with unit cost.
  • Later & partly data-blocked: MEIO (network safety-stock placement), capacitated scheduling / distribution planning (LP/MILP).
9 Why there is no LP/MILP here

Single-echelon inventory policy has closed-form / empirical solutions; optimization is for constrained problems, later.

The v1 engine is a quantile read off backtest residuals — no solver. LP/MILP becomes justified only when items share constraints or there are combinatorial trade-offs:
